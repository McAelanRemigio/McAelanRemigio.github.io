Workforce Analytics & Operations Strategy

Developed analytical infrastructure and strategic reporting systems at San Diego State University Career Services, supporting workforce development, institutional planning, program evaluation, and operational decision-making.

Organizational context

Career Services operates across multiple service lines, programs, employer relationships, and student engagement channels. Each generates operational information that can inform institutional planning and resource allocation.

However, information was distributed across different systems, exports, reporting conventions, and service areas. This created challenges for reconciling activity, maintaining consistent KPI definitions, and developing a unified understanding of organizational performance.

My role focused on addressing these challenges through analytical infrastructure and strategic reporting: making operational information more consistent, accessible, and useful to leadership.

Workforce strategy & labor market intelligence

Supported a statewide California State University workforce development initiative through regional labor market analysis and program research. The work examined workforce demand, regional labor gaps, and alignment between educational programming and industry needs across four CSU campuses.

These findings contributed to a successful $2M initiative, supporting workforce development planning and resource allocation.

Enterprise reporting infrastructure

Reconstructed two years of fragmented program lifecycle information, spanning 761K+ records across more than 100 files, into standardized analytical datasets and repeatable reporting workflows.

This involved resolving inconsistencies across operational exports, establishing common data structures, documenting business rules, and improving the reliability of recurring reporting processes.

Strategic value

The broader contribution was creating a stronger connection between operational activity and institutional decision-making. Rather than treating reporting as an isolated administrative task, the work established a foundation for understanding organizational performance, identifying patterns, and supporting more informed planning.

Why this matters

Organizations do not make better decisions simply because they collect more data. They make better decisions when information is reliable, consistently defined, and connected to the questions leadership needs to answer.

This experience reinforced my interest in the intersection of strategy, operations, and analytics: building the infrastructure that allows organizations to move beyond fragmented reporting toward more informed planning and execution.
